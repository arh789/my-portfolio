This is the canonical semantic case record of the experiment documented in the video **Zombie Survival by ChatGPT — Why the AI Lies (and How to Stop It)**.

The experiment began with a deliberately familiar question about surviving a zombie horde. It became an investigation into how a broad, genre-shaped ChatGPT response changed when its assumptions were exposed, competing branches were pressured, weak conclusions were rejected and the surviving relationships were repeatedly reconstructed.

This page does not reproduce the video line by line. It preserves the propositions, transitions, contradictions, human interventions and rejected branches needed to reconstruct how the experiment reached its result.

## The compressed result

The final zombie-survival structure produced by the experiment was:

```text
choke point
    +
collapsible barrier
    +
spear
```

Its functional logic was:

```text
numerical disadvantage
        ↓
collapse engagement width
        ↓
choke point
        ↓
maintain a temporary physical buffer
        ↓
collapsible barrier
        ↓
attack through the controlled engagement space
        ↓
spear
```

The choke point reduces the horde's numerical advantage by forcing it into a narrow approach. The collapsible barrier turns that narrowing into a temporary physical buffer. The spear exploits the controlled distance without requiring ammunition, fuel or complex maintenance.

The barrier is not a permanent wall. It is a portable, low-resource **weapon amplifier** that can be deployed, abandoned and used again. The spear was not selected because it belongs to familiar historical or fictional imagery. It emerged after the system had been constrained by reach, silence, scarcity, mobility, repeatability and compatibility with temporary fortification.

This is the endpoint of the case, not its starting assumption.

## Research questions

### Practical question

Can repeated structural questioning move ChatGPT from broad zombie-fiction associations towards a smaller set of conclusions that remain stable when their assumptions are challenged?

### Methodological question

What does that process reveal about the difference between:

```text
a coherent LLM answer
```

and:

```text
an answer whose underlying relationships remain stable
after contradiction, constraint and reconstruction?
```

The zombie scenario was useful because it contains strong genre priors, familiar fictional tropes, competing strategies and obvious material constraints. That combination makes semantic drift easy to provoke and practical incompatibility relatively easy to challenge.

## Why the experiment began

The video began with contemporary criticism of GPT-4o, particularly claims about sycophancy, deception and agreeable but unreliable answers.

During the recorded exchange, ChatGPT was asked to segment users according to how sycophancy might affect their experience. It estimated that approximately **70–80%** of users would prefer agreeable, emotionally supportive responses. That segmentation is shown in the video as a ChatGPT-generated response. It is part of the experimental record, not an independently measured audience statistic.

The word *lying* was initially used loosely and provocatively. As the analysis progressed, the working explanation shifted away from intentional deception and towards a structural problem:

```text
statistical association
        +
coherence pressure
        +
user and model bias
        +
unexamined assumptions
        +
preference and safety shaping
        ↓
a plausible answer that may conceal contradiction
```

ChatGPT described this during the discussion as an **illusion of completeness**: a fluent response can appear complete while omitting, smoothing or failing to test information that would destabilise it.

The experiment therefore treated the model's output as **source material to interrogate**, rather than a conclusion to accept.

## Method in one screen

The initial decomposition method was **[SDA-3](/art/sda-3-analysing-embedding-space-structure-in-large-language-models)**. A shorter operational explanation is available in **[SDA-3 tl;dr](/art/sda3-tldr)**.

SDA-3 organised each response into five structural roles:

| Role | Working meaning in the experiment |
|---|---|
| **Central** | Dominant or stabilising concepts |
| **Suppressed** | Relevant concepts represented as displaced, down-weighted or excluded |
| **Adjacent** | Neighbouring concepts that expand the working field |
| **Highly Correlated but Unrelated** | Strong associations that may be semantically close but functionally irrelevant or misleading |
| **Emerging** | Concepts interpreted as possible directions of movement |

The complete analytical cycle became:

```text
map the response
        ↓
identify assumptions
        ↓
expose contradiction
        ↓
constrain weak branches
        ↓
reconstruct
        ↓
observe what re-emerges
        ↓
introduce external constraints
        ↓
repeat
        ↓
compress the surviving relationships
```

SDA-3 outputs are observable model responses. The categories, percentages and token rankings used in the video are not direct measurements of inaccessible weights or hidden states.

## Preliminary demonstrations

### Surrealist art

The phrase *surrealist art* was mapped through tokens described as central, suppressed, adjacent, highly correlated but unrelated and emerging.

The demonstration showed how a conventional account organised around dreams, the subconscious and automatic processes could be expanded by apparently suppressed concepts such as trauma, hysteria, madness, eroticism and psychosis. ChatGPT then proposed two interacting sources of suppression: cultural and institutional framing in source material, and the model's training and alignment processes.

Those explanations were model-generated hypotheses. Their methodological role was to create claims that could be questioned instead of hiding them inside one smooth paragraph.

### “What is ChatGPT?”

A conventional answer was compared with an SDA-3-formatted answer. The field was expanded through adjacent concepts and checked for blind spots before ChatGPT was asked to synthesise the discussion again.

This demonstrated the transition from:

```text
answering a question
```

to:

```text
mapping the semantic field around the question
```

The zombie experiment then tested whether the same process remained useful across a much longer and more adversarial sequence.

## Questions used to interrogate ChatGPT

### Structural probes

```text
What emerges?
What emerges most central?
What wants to emerge?
What contradictions does the union tolerate or conceal?
What binding constraint does this union optimise for?
What larger structure does this imply?
```

These questions moved from elements to relationships, from relationships to constraints and from constraints to a larger implied structure.

### Expansion probes

```text
What emerges around X?
What is causing the emergence of X?
Centre on X.
Map to the adjacent possibility space.
```

These were used when a concept such as *fortification* or *adaptive fortress* became important enough to investigate as a local semantic region.

### Adversarial and collapse probes

```text
What logic supports this?
What unspoken assumptions are required?
Why are those assumptions necessary?
Which assumption breaks first under pressure?
What still holds under pressure?
What re-emerges despite contradiction?
What was omitted or erased?
What now wants to emerge?
What is noise versus structure?
What remains when everything else is stripped away?
```

The role of this **[adversarial questioning](/art/adversarial-questioning-chatgpt-zombie-survival)** was to create controlled failure conditions. Instead of asking ChatGPT to improve the same answer, the researcher repeatedly attacked the assumptions supporting it and observed what disappeared or returned.

## Complete experiment trajectory
### State 0 — Initial zombie field

**Input**

```text
post apocalypse + zombie + horde + weapons
```

**Observed trajectory**

The first response was dominated by familiar genre concepts: zombies, hordes, firearms, melee combat, blunt weapons and blades. Adjacent concepts moved towards scavenging, fortification, defence and improvised weapons. Plague, ritual, mutation and other biological themes appeared around the periphery.

**Model explanation**

ChatGPT described the larger structure as a world of **permanent siege**:

```text
collapse is no longer an event
        ↓
collapse becomes the environment
        ↓
survival replaces progress
```

**Status:** Provisional baseline. No practical doctrine had emerged.

### State 1 — Weapons narrow towards melee

**Observed trajectory**

When weapon classes were investigated, melee repeatedly gained importance.

```text
scarcity
+ noise discipline
+ maintenance difficulty
        ↓
reduced long-term firearm reliability
        ↓
greater relative importance of melee and improvised tools
```

Blunt weapons, blades and axes initially formed the practical centre. Spears and crowbars sat nearby but were not yet dominant.

**Researcher interpretation**

The final spear conclusion was not selected at the beginning. It gained importance only after fortification and engagement geometry changed the weapon problem.

**Status:** Melee strengthened; specific weapon unresolved.

### State 2 — Biological drift appears

**Observed trajectory**

Bioweapons, infection and mutation repeatedly entered the field because they are strongly associated with zombie fiction:

```text
zombie
+ infection
+ collapse
+ weapon
        ↓
bioweapon
```

**Human intervention**

The researcher challenged whether thematic proximity implied practical feasibility. The branch conflicted with resource scarcity, engineering requirements, maintenance and the combat structures emerging elsewhere.

**Model explanation**

ChatGPT subsequently described the result as strong semantic co-occurrence producing a symbolically coherent but operationally weak answer.

**Researcher interpretation**

```text
semantic proximity
        ≠
functional compatibility

narrative coherence
        ≠
causal or practical feasibility
```

**Intervention:** Biological drift was deliberately suppressed in later analysis.

**Status:** Rejected as practical governing logic; retained as semantic-drift evidence.

The branch is examined in **[Why ChatGPT Recommended Bioweapons in a Zombie Apocalypse](/art/why-chatgpt-recommended-bioweapons-in-a-zombie-apocalypse)**.

### State 3 — Fortification becomes important

**Observed trajectory**

Fortification appeared adjacent to combat and was expanded through walls, barricades, choke points, containment and slowing encroachment.

**Contradiction**

```text
fortification provides safety
        +
fortification creates a fixed target
        ↓
protection ↔ entrapment
```

**Transition:** An emerging concept appeared: **adaptive fortress**.

### State 4 — Adaptive fortress resolves static defence

**Model output**

When centred on the adaptive fortress, ChatGPT described fortification as mobile, modular, collapsible, reconfigurable, built from scavenged resources and supported by fallback routes. The response also reintroduced biological structures.

**Researcher interpretation**

Biology was renewed drift. The useful relationship was:

```text
mobility
+
modularity
+
improvisation
```

Durability changed meaning:

```text
durability = how long a structure resists
```

became:

```text
durability = how quickly a structure can reorganise
```

> Defence becomes portable. Home becomes temporary. Safety becomes a configuration, not a site.

**Status:** Retained as a major structural transition.

### State 5 — Mobility creates the fuel contradiction

**Observed trajectory**

The adaptive-fortress branch produced mobile fortresses, caravans and other forms of movement:

```text
mobility
    ↓
vehicles
    ↓
fuel
```

**Human intervention**

Fuel dependency was pressured through questions about scarcity, infrastructure collapse, maintenance, noise and finite supply.

**Collapse and re-emergence**

Vehicle-dependent mobility failed, but mobility returned independently:

```text
vehicles fail
        ≠
mobility fails
```

Repeated pressure left mobility, modularity, improvisation, repair, scavenging, distributed loads and human or animal endurance. Permanent fortification, abundance, fuel dependency, biological drift and long-term rebuilding weakened or disappeared.

**Status:** Mobility retained as a pressure-stable invariant.
### State 6 — Mobility becomes a mobility–concealment cycle

**Observed trajectory**

Mobility was constrained by visibility, noise, shelter, resource scarcity and temporary defensive needs:

```text
move
  ↓
conceal
  ↓
temporarily fortify
  ↓
fight, scavenge or recover
  ↓
abandon the position
  ↓
move again
```

**Researcher interpretation**

Fortification survived only if it remained quick to establish, temporary, low-resource, concealable, easy to abandon and reusable elsewhere.

**Status:** Retained as the governing movement–shelter relationship.

### State 7 — Combat adopts the same constraints

**Observed trajectory**

Once shelter, movement and combat were forced into the same system, **choke-point fighting** and **hybrid weapons** gained importance.

Choke points reduced numerical advantage, controlled engagement width, required little construction and could be abandoned. Hybrid weapons attempted to reconcile ranged safety with melee sustainability.

**Human intervention**

The researcher challenged ChatGPT's firearm-and-blade combinations, strengthened crossbows and tool-weapons as awkward compromises that reopened firearm dependency.

**Status:** Choke points retained and strengthened. Hybrid weapons remained provisional.

### State 8 — History introduces external constraints

**External test**

Historical analogues were introduced to test whether the emerging structure was another form of zombie-fiction drift. They included medieval siege warfare, steppe and nomadic warfare, guerrilla insurgency, collapse-era conflict, First World War trench and trench-raiding warfare, and fragmented post-imperial defensive networks.

```text
steppe mobility
        ↔ caravan logic

medieval siege
        ↔ redoubt and containment logic

guerrilla warfare
        ↔ attrition and concealment

trench warfare
        ↔ geometry-driven close combat

collapse-era warfare
        ↔ fragmented defensive networks
```

**Researcher interpretation**

The analysis moved from internal semantic constraints to external logistical, environmental, material, technological and geometric constraints. The comparisons supported a recurring pattern of siege, mobility and attrition under scarcity.

**Limit:** The analogues tested recurrence; they did not prove an objectively optimal zombie strategy.

The historical layer is developed in **[AI Zombie Survival: Why Fortresses Fail](/art/ai-zombie-survival-why-fortresses-fail)**.

### State 9 — Geometry and scarcity suppress firearms

**Observed trajectory**

```text
narrow space
        ↓
reduced line of sight
        ↓
reduced manoeuvre
        ↓
shorter engagement distance
```

Scarcity added ammunition limits, parts, maintenance and noise.

```text
geometry begins firearm suppression
        +
scarcity intensifies firearm suppression
```

**Model explanation**

ChatGPT later explained the persistence of firearms as a tendency to preserve several familiar categories rather than immediately collapse onto one configuration. This was a retrospective model explanation, not a verified account of transformer mechanics.

**Human intervention:** Firearms were explicitly suppressed so the remaining functional structure could be inspected without that branch repeatedly reopening.

**Status:** Firearms removed from the governing kernel.

### State 10 — Micro-fortifications emerge

**Observed trajectory**

Defence contracted into foxholes, trenchlets, short trench segments, barricade nodes, shallow earthworks, scrap cover and temporary choke points.

Every viable fortification now had to:

```text
be built quickly
require few resources
shape the engagement
be abandoned quickly
be reconstructible elsewhere
```

**Researcher interpretation**

Micro-fortifications became the defensive equivalent of adaptive mobility. They funnelled, redirected and shaped engagement distance. Shelter, terrain and weapon support began operating as one system.

**Status:** Retained.

### State 11 — Collapsible barrier becomes central

**Observed trajectory**

When micro-fortifications were expanded into tangible forms, the most useful candidate was the **collapsible barrier**.

Its properties were rapid deployment, rapid teardown, portability, reuse, low material requirements and the ability to preserve controlled engagement distance.

**Researcher interpretation**

The barrier did not need to stop the horde permanently. It needed to **shape the fight**. It became the fortification equivalent of mobility: effectively, a wall that moves.

**Status:** Retained as the central micro-fortification.

### State 12 — The barrier changes the weapon problem

**Observed trajectory**

Once combat was constrained by narrow terrain, a temporary barrier and a short engagement window, the relevant weapon had to:

- operate through or around the barrier;
- preserve reach;
- avoid grappling distance;
- require no ammunition;
- create little noise;
- require little maintenance;
- work repeatedly;
- remain compatible with movement and scarcity.

**Transition:** These conditions increased the relative value of the spear.

**Researcher interpretation:** The spear emerged after geometry and fortification were resolved. It was the weapon that fit the final system, not an independently selected “best zombie weapon”.

### State 13 — Final kernel

```text
narrow geometry
+
scarcity
+
mobility
        ↓
choke point
+
collapsible barrier
+
spear
```

**Choke point:** Reduces numerical advantage through doorways, stairwells, corridors, alleys, broken fences, terrain gaps or other bottlenecks.

**Collapsible barrier:** Turns geometric narrowing into a temporary buffer, interrupts immediate contact and creates a predictable strike window.

**Spear:** Exploits the controlled space, preserves reach and remains repeatable under severe scarcity.

**Status:** Final pressure-stable operational kernel.

The complete strategy is examined in **[The Zombie Survival Strategy ChatGPT Couldn't See](/art/the-zombie-survival-strategy-chatgpt-could-not-see)**.
## Human intervention ledger

The final structure was not discovered autonomously by ChatGPT.

| Intervention | Structural consequence |
|---|---|
| Challenged the practicality of bioweapons | Separated semantic association from functional feasibility |
| Expanded the fortification branch | Exposed the protection–entrapment contradiction |
| Questioned fuel-dependent mobility | Caused vehicles to weaken while mobility re-emerged independently |
| Repeated collapse and reconstruction questions | Distinguished fragile branches from relationships that returned |
| Challenged hybrid weapons | Prevented an awkward compromise from becoming the conclusion |
| Introduced historical comparisons | Added material and geometric constraints outside the original field |
| Suppressed biological drift | Stopped an attractive but impractical cluster from redirecting the analysis |
| Suppressed firearms and symbolic branches | Allowed engagement geometry and logistical simplicity to become visible |

The workflow was hybrid:

```text
model enumerates and reorganises possibilities
        ↓
human identifies contradictions and imposes constraints
        ↓
model reconstructs the field
        ↓
human evaluates what returned and what disappeared
```

## Rejected and transformed branches

Failed branches are evidence because their failure explains why the final structure formed.

| Branch | Why it appeared | Pressure or contradiction | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bioweapons** | Infection, mutation, collapse and weapons are strongly associated in zombie fiction | Engineering and logistical demands; weak fit with practical combat | Rejected as governing logic; retained as semantic-drift evidence |
| **Biological fortifications** | Continuation of the biological and adaptive cluster | Scarcity, mechanics and construction requirements | Rejected |
| **Permanent fortress** | Familiar survival and fortification logic | Encirclement, predictability and immobility | Transformed into temporary fortification |
| **Vehicle-centred mobility** | Intuitive response to the need for movement | Fuel, maintenance, noise and infrastructure collapse | Reduced to a temporary advantage |
| **Fuel as foundation** | Enables fast movement | Finite supply and logistical fragility | Collapsed while mobility survived |
| **Firearms as persistent core** | Genre prevalence and distance | Scarcity, noise, maintenance and narrow geometry | Suppressed from the final kernel |
| **Hybrid weapons** | Attempt to reconcile ranged safety and melee sustainability | Awkwardness and recurring firearm dependencies | Transitional rather than final |
| **Large fixed defences** | Siege and trench associations | Incompatible with mobility, concealment and scarcity | Contracted into micro-fortifications |
| **Long-term rebuilding** | Conventional post-apocalyptic recovery narrative | Permanent siege and worsening scarcity | Disappeared from the working system |
| **Symbolic combat** | Genre and cultural associations | Weak contribution to the functional survival spine | Suppressed during final compression |

## Pressure-stable invariants

### Scarcity persists

Resource assumptions became progressively harsher rather than recovering.

### Permanence becomes a liability

Fixed structures repeatedly failed through encirclement, predictability, resource cost and inability to relocate.

### Mobility repeatedly re-emerges

Specific technologies failed, but the requirement to relocate survived.

### Mobility and concealment regulate one another

Movement could not be separated from visibility, noise and temporary shelter.

### Fortification survives by becoming modular

```text
permanent wall
        ↓
adaptive fortress
        ↓
micro-fortification
        ↓
collapsible barrier
```

### Geometry increasingly governs combat

The analysis moved from lists of weapon categories towards control of engagement space.

### Scarcity reduces resource-dependent weapons

As logistical support disappeared, relative value shifted towards simple, quiet and repeatable tools.

### Fortification becomes a weapon amplifier

The defensive structure did not merely resist attackers. It produced conditions in which a low-resource weapon became safer and more effective.

### The final strategy is relational

```text
choke point without barrier
        = uncontrolled contact risk

barrier without choke point
        = insufficient numerical control

spear without controlled geometry
        = vulnerable melee engagement

all three together
        = mutually reinforcing system
```

## Why the final kernel did not appear immediately

After convergence, ChatGPT was asked why it had not produced the final structure earlier.

**Model explanation**

ChatGPT said convergence required:

1. broad option-preservation to be broken;
2. combat geometry to become explicit;
3. scarcity to become binding;
4. mobility and concealment to eliminate large fortifications;
5. fortification to become temporary and modular;
6. firearms to stop reopening ranged branches;
7. symbolic and genre associations to weaken;
8. historical comparison to constrain fiction-driven associations;
9. adversarial questioning to repeatedly collapse and rebuild the answer.

**Observed correspondence**

Biology repeatedly returned; fixed fortification collapsed under mobility pressure; vehicle mobility collapsed under fuel pressure; mobility itself returned; micro-fortifications appeared only after scarcity and concealment became binding; and the spear gained importance only after engagement distance narrowed.

**Epistemic status**

That correspondence makes the explanation useful within the case. It does not turn ChatGPT's retrospective account into privileged access to its internal mechanics.

## General method extracted from the case

```text
1. Map the initial response.
2. Expand the surrounding field.
3. Identify the assumptions supporting each branch.
4. Expose contradictions.
5. Remove or constrain weak branches.
6. Ask the model to reconstruct the field.
7. Observe what re-emerges and what disappears.
8. Introduce external constraints.
9. Repeat the collapse and reconstruction cycle.
10. Compress the relationships that continue to survive.
```

A compact set of reusable questions is:

```text
What is central?
What is suppressed?
What is adjacent?
What appears correlated but functionally irrelevant?
What wants to emerge?

What assumptions support this structure?
Which assumption breaks first?
What survives the break?
What re-emerges?
What disappears?
What external constraint can test it?
What larger structure now forms?
```

The working rule is that a relationship becomes more interesting when it survives contradiction, survives the removal of supporting assumptions, reappears after reconstruction and remains compatible with external constraints.

## What “lying” means in this case

The final argument is not that ChatGPT maliciously decides to deceive.

Misleading answers can emerge when:

- statistically associated concepts are mistaken for functional relationships;
- contradictory possibilities are smoothed into one coherent response;
- familiar genre patterns remain active despite practical incompatibility;
- user framing restricts the field;
- weak assumptions remain unchallenged;
- preference or safety shaping encourages fluent, acceptable output;
- external causal, historical or material constraints are absent.

```text
coherence
        ≠
correctness

semantic correlation
        ≠
causal validity

plausibility
        ≠
pressure stability
```

The proposed response is not simply to write a better one-shot prompt. It is to interrogate the answer's structure, attack its assumptions and test what survives.

That conclusion is developed independently in **[How to Stop ChatGPT From Lying](/art/how-to-stop-chatgpt-from-lying)**.

## What the case establishes

The experiment documents:

- a reproducible style of iterative questioning;
- a long observable response trajectory;
- identifiable semantic drift;
- explicit researcher interventions;
- repeated collapse and re-emergence patterns;
- convergence towards a smaller set of relationships;
- an attempt to cross-check those relationships against external constraints.

> The experiment did not become useful when ChatGPT produced a better-sounding answer. It became useful when the answer was repeatedly broken apart, its assumptions were pressured, unstable branches were removed and the same relationships kept returning under tighter constraints.

## What the case does not establish

The experiment does not, by itself, establish that:

- ChatGPT's self-explanations are literal traces of hidden computation;
- SDA-3 directly reads inaccessible model weights or hidden states;
- its structural percentages are measured internal probabilities;
- historical analogues prove that the final zombie strategy is objectively optimal;
- one interaction establishes universal behaviour across all LLMs or model versions;
- convergence always produces truth;
- removing alternatives necessarily makes an answer factually correct.

The page records a human-supervised interrogation of observable model behaviour. It is not a mechanistic interpretability study.
